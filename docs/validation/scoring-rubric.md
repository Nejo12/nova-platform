# Vertical Scoring Rubric

Score each dimension from 1–5 using interview evidence, not intuition.

## Pain frequency

1 — rare / exceptional  
2 — occasional  
3 — recurring monthly  
4 — recurring weekly  
5 — daily or present in most enquiries

## Pain severity

1 — minor inconvenience  
2 — noticeable but easy workaround  
3 — regular wasted time / friction  
4 — lost leads, repeated manual work or customer confusion  
5 — directly harms revenue, capacity or customer experience

## Existing software spend

1 — effectively €0  
2 — < €10/month  
3 — €10–€30/month  
4 — €30–€100/month  
5 — > €100/month or multiple overlapping tools

## Lead / booking value

1 — low-value transaction  
2 — modest  
3 — meaningful  
4 — high-value  
5 — very high-value / a single conversion can justify months of software fees

## One-page suitability

1 — requires complex multi-page information architecture  
2 — substantial multi-page need  
3 — could work with constraints  
4 — strong fit  
5 — near-perfect fit for portfolio → trust → enquiry

## Customer reachability

1 — difficult to identify or contact  
2 — fragmented / gated  
3 — reachable with effort  
4 — clear communities/channels  
5 — highly reachable through social/directories/associations

## Competitive intensity

Reverse scored:

1 — dominant incumbents solve this extremely well  
2 — heavily crowded  
3 — competitive but differentiated wedge exists  
4 — weak/siloed alternatives  
5 — obvious unmet gap

## Structured workflow opportunity

1 — little repeatable workflow  
2 — mostly bespoke  
3 — some common structure  
4 — strongly repeatable  
5 — highly standardized across the profession

## Payment intent

1 — expects free  
2 — reluctant to pay  
3 — plausible at low price  
4 — clear willingness around target price  
5 — existing spend + explicit willingness to switch/pay

## Expansion potential

1 — isolated utility  
2 — narrow feature ceiling  
3 — adjacent capability potential  
4 — clear path to lead/booking/business workflows  
5 — strong vertical SaaS expansion opportunity

## Decision guidance

Do not automatically choose the highest arithmetic score.

A launch vertical should also have:
- at least one repeated, severe problem;
- a clear acquisition channel;
- a credible conversion action;
- evidence that Tessnova can solve the problem without becoming a huge product.
